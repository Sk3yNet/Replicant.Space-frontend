Relay network

Your working FTL relays (7.5 ly, or the range a relay reports) and active system hubs (15 ly) link when they're within reach of each other; linked ones form a network, each in its own color here and on the galaxy map (relay network option). Other players' relays don't join yours. A relay must sit at an L4/L5 point; it may go in a warded system. A network is active when one of your replicants is within its reach: only then can devices it covers be commanded. Anything delivered beyond an active network rides a vessel hosting a replicant, or isn't sent.

Relay rollout

A relay in every system, nearest your seed systems first: as many relay orders stay open as your logistics vessels with a replicant aboard have free hold slots (as add on the Systems list: an idle one there is switched on, else a spare is sent, else one is printed in range), and each one that's up or dropped makes room for the next. A carrier takes as many relays as its hold has room for and drops them system by system, nearest first. Chained: only a system within 7.5 ly of a working relay or hub of yours, one on order, or a seed comes next, so the network grows outward in one piece. A system that can't get one is skipped until you retry; after five in a row the rollout pauses. Pause also cancels every relay print not started yet (queued ones are dequeued, orders waiting on a print are dropped) and no relay is printed for any order, the Systems list's included, until you start again; a print already running finishes. Each seed reaches as far as its own radius (the default until you change it; a new seed can be typed as STAR:ly), and a system counts for the nearest seed whose radius it lies in.
